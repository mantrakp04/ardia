Root-associated fungi shift nitrogen uptake in drought-stressed beech seedlings

Ingrid Halden1, Mateo Kallas1, Priya Ventresca1,2, Oskar Lunde1, Yerlan Tusupov3
1 Halden Lab, Department of Soil Ecology, Marrow Hill Institute · 2 Centre for Forest Genomics · 3 Northfield Arboretum

Abstract

Ectomycorrhizal fungi are thought to buffer trees against drought, but how they change nutrient uptake as soils dry is poorly resolved. We grew 240 beech seedlings with or without native root fungi under three watering regimes and traced 15N uptake for eight weeks. Under moderate drought, colonised seedlings took up 2.3× more labelled nitrogen and kept 31% more leaf area than sterile controls, while the advantage disappeared under severe drought. ITS sequencing of 96 root tips showed that colonisation shifted towards long-distance exploration types as soil moisture fell.
