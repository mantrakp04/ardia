Canopy cover and street-level heat: how mature street trees cool summer sidewalks across 212 city blocks

Maren Ostrowe1, Tobias Ekwueme1,2, Lucía Ferrand-Hale2, Idris Kanbara3, Hanne Solvik1✉

Abstract

Street trees are the cheapest air conditioner a city owns, but how much shade a block needs before pedestrians feel the difference is still argued. We paired 2,140 hand-held sidewalk readings with a tree-by-tree census across 212 blocks of a mid-sized coastal city during three heat waves. Blocks above 40% canopy were on average 4.1 °C cooler at 15:00 than blocks below 10%, and surface temperatures of shaded asphalt fell by up to 19 °C. Cooling rose steeply up to roughly 35% cover and then levelled off, suggesting that planting gaps on bare blocks returns more relief per tree than thickening already-shaded ones.

Keywordsstreet treesurban heat islandcanopy coverthermal comfortsidewalk temperaturegreen infrastructure

1Introduction

Heat is the deadliest weather in most temperate cities, and it is unevenly shared. Blocks with fewer trees tend to be blocks with more asphalt, older housing and fewer people who can leave town when the forecast turns. Municipal planting programmes have grown quickly since 2020, yet most still set targets by city-wide canopy percentage rather than by where shade is missing [4, 11, 17].

Here we ask a narrower question that planners can act on: on a single block, how much canopy does it take before a person walking the sidewalk at three in the afternoon measurably feels cooler, and does each additional tree keep helping?

2Study area and data

Readings were taken on 212 blocks in Port Alder between 1 July and 28 August 2025, a summer that included three heat waves of four or more days above 31 °C.
