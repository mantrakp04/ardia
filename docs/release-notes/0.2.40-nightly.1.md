## New

- **Search for any setting.** A field at the top of the Settings sidebar finds individual settings by name, description or a common word for them ("cookies", "dark", "pip"), and forgives a typo. ⌘F jumps to it, ↑↓ pick a result, and Return opens the pane with the setting scrolled into view and briefly highlighted, as in System Settings.
- **The sidebar's top row sits where Arc's does.** With the address bar in the sidebar, the window buttons and the URL field line up with Arc's from the first frame. The sidebar's buttons take clicks only on their own plates; the rest of the row moves the window, and a double-click there zooms it.

## Fixed

- **⌘V and typing right after ⌘T, ⌘L or ⌘F land in the new field.** A paste or the first letters typed just after opening a tab, the address bar or Find went into the old page, so you had to press ⌘V twice or type again. They now wait for the new field and land there.
- **Escape and ⌘W give the page its focus back.** Closing the command bar left nothing focused, so keys and ⌘V did nothing until you clicked the page.
- **Launch goes straight from the saved picture to the real window.** For a moment after launch the window went dark, with only its buttons, before the sidebar appeared. The picture shown while Ardia starts also had a white address bar and white pinned tiles; it now looks like the window it stands in for.
- **Settings panes open as they are.** Switching panes showed toggles sliding to their real values, lists popping in late and everything shifted a few points to the side for a frame. Each pane now appears finished.
- **The pinned tiles stay put while the tabs scroll.** The tiles used to scroll with the tabs and slide under the address field. Now the tabs scroll under a thin line that fades in once the list moves, as in Dia.
- **Peeking at a hidden sidebar shows no sliver of the page.** A thin edge of the page showed round the sidebar's corners when it slid in from the left edge.
